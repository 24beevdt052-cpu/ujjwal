## 1. What this is, and what "formula-driven" means here

MASTER_SPEC Table 8.2 asks for a master Excel workbook that is formula-driven for Components 1–3, with GARCH, Monte Carlo and credit scoring left in Python. This is that workbook. Because "formula-driven" is a claim a recruiter can test in thirty seconds by changing a cell, it is worth being exact about what is live and what is not:

* **Components 1 and 2 are fully live.** `Parity`, both Table 1.5 sensitivity grids and the Table 1.7 term structure contain **no computed constants at all** — every line item is an Excel formula reading `Inputs` and `Market_Weekly`/`Market_Daily`. The trade tickets and counterparties are inputs, which is what they are in `config/` too; the CONTRACTS §5a eligibility of each ticket is re-tested live against `Parity`.
* **Component 3 is live at the leg level on a declared marking grid.** The FX conversion of every dated flow, the whole MCX variation- and initial-margin schedule, the FX-forward marks, the unsold-cargo replacement mark, per-trade cumulative P&L and the book equity curve are formulas. Leg *amounts* that fall out of the ticket lifecycle are carried from the engine, labelled green and named row by row in `amount_src` / `value_src`; §7 lists them and says why each one stays in Python.
* **Nothing is a picture.** The equity curve is a native Excel line chart over formula cells.

Window 2022-03-01 → 2022-08-31; engine horizon 2022-10-31; panel 2022-01-04 → 2022-12-30 (251 LME trading days).

## 2. What this does and doesn't tell you

**It does** let you interrogate the desk's arithmetic without reading Python: change a duty rate, a grade factor, a freight level or an LME print and watch the import parity, the trade-eligibility flags, the marks and the P&L move together, with every intermediate line visible. It also shows, cell by cell, that the workbook and the engine agree — the `Checks` sheet differences them at every grain the workbook reaches.

**It does not** make the numbers more true. A formula inherits the provenance of its inputs: the freight levels and the lag-2 grade mix are hindsight reconstructions (CONTRACTS §4.3, `scrap_grades.yaml`), the USD/INR series is an ECB cross standing in for the RBI/FBIL reference rate, and the MCX series is duty-paid import parity — which is exactly why the cross-exchange basis on this book is **zero by construction** and the workbook shows that residual as a column rather than claiming a basis it cannot observe. It is also not a risk system: a spreadsheet that recalculates one state cannot produce a GARCH volatility path or a 10,000-path P&L distribution, and this one does not pretend to.

## 5. Using it in an interview

Open `Checks` first — the scoreboard at the top is a formula over every block below it, so it cannot be stale, and it tells the reader in one line whether the workbook still agrees with the engine. Then:

| Do this | And it shows |
|---|---|
| Prove the parity is live | Inputs → change `bcd_scrap_hs7602` from 0.025 to 0.05. Watch Parity `bcd_inr_t`, `landed_inr_t` and `net_arb_inr_t` move on all 258 rows, `trade_eligible` flip on the marginal weeks, and Trade_Book `parity_eligible_live` follow. Then undo it — Checks will go red until you do, which is the point. |
| Prove the P&L is live | Market_Daily → change a `usdinr` print. Cashflows re-converts every USD flow settling that day, MTM_Daily re-prices the replacement mark and the FX forwards, and the Equity_Curve chart redraws. |
| Show the hedge is not a fake | Attribution §3a: the short MCX hedge on the day the LME broke from its all-time high. (a) is the whole move, (b) the cross-exchange basis is **zero**, (e) is small and negative because a short MCX position is short USD, and (g) is the carry. §3b shows a USD payable and its forward cancelling in (e) and (g). |
| Show the discipline | Trade_Book `parity_eligible_live` vs `book_trade_eligible`: each ticket is re-tested against the CONTRACTS §5a rule that was declared before any Phase 1 result existed. |
| Show the honesty | Checks → the scoreboard, then Inputs §4: the Phase 3 parameters still served from code rather than from the register, listed with their flags. |

The single best demonstration is the first one: `bcd_scrap_hs7602` 0.025 → 0.05 moves the duty base on all 258 parity rows, pushes `net_arb_inr_t` down by roughly the duty on the assessable value, flips `trade_eligible` on the marginal weeks, and turns `Trade_Book!eligible_matches_book` red for any ticket whose week no longer clears — which is the CONTRACTS §5a discipline rule enforcing itself in front of the interviewer. Undo the change and everything goes green again.

Each row of each block pastes the engine's value (green), references the workbook's own formula cell, shows the difference and raises a PASS/FAIL against a tolerance held as a named range on `Inputs`. ## 7. Reconciliation test

`tests/test_excel_reconciliation.py` loads the saved workbook with the `formulas` library and **recalculates it from the formulas alone**. There is nothing else it could do: openpyxl writes formulas without cached results, and one of the tests opens the saved zip and asserts that no `<f>` element is followed by a cached `<v>`. It then asserts four things:

1. every `check` cell recalculates to PASS, and so does the scoreboard formula that aggregates them;
2. the computed sheets really are computed — a formula-vs-constant census per sheet against a floor per sheet, so a later edit cannot quietly swap a formula for a pasted number;
3. no cell recalculates to `#REF!`, `#NAME?`, `#DIV/0!`, `#VALUE!`, `#N/A`, `#NULL!` or `#NUM!`;
4. the workbook is byte-for-byte deterministic (CONTRACTS §1.5) — rebuilding it produces an identical file, which is why the zip entry timestamps and the document properties are pinned.

## 8. What stays in Python, and why

| Left in Python | Why |
|---|---|
| GARCH(1,1) VaR, the 10,000-path Monte Carlo, logistic credit scoring | MASTER_SPEC Table 8.2 places them in Python. A spreadsheet version would be a different, worse model wearing the same name. |
| The eight-step sequential attribution, per trade-day | Eight full revaluations of every leg of every ticket, every day. Worked live on two closed-form legs instead (`Attribution` §3a, §3b), and the published table's identities are re-checked in cells. |
| Leg amounts out of the ticket lifecycle | Quotational-period averages, survey outcomes and claim arithmetic, demurrage day counts, LC fee bases. Carried green and marked `PYTHON_LIFECYCLE`; the market conversion applied to them is a formula. |
| Funding accrual | A day-by-day walk of each trade's own dated cash balance at `wc_rate_inr_pa`, including variation margin and the balance-sheet flows. Carried and labelled on `Equity_Curve` and `MTM_Daily`. |
| Every panel day of leg-level MTM | `mtm_daily.csv` is 28,343 rows. The workbook values every open leg on a declared marking grid — month-end panel days, every trade date, the adverse-event endpoints, `WINDOW_END`, `HORIZON_END` — fixed as a rule in `desk.excel.sources`, not chosen after seeing results. |
| Excel Data Tables for the sensitivity grids | No Python library can evaluate a Data Table, so a Data Table would be a number nobody could check. Each grid cell instead re-derives the whole CONTRACTS §5 build-up at the shocked market with explicit formulas. |

Two of these deserve the longer answer.

**The attribution chain.** The engine splits each day's P&L by fully revaluing every leg of every ticket eight times — once for each `FACTOR_ORDER` block, then for the contracts block — from yesterday's state to today's, so that cross-terms land in the factor that moves last and the residual is a genuine control rather than a plug. Reproducing that in cells would mean eight copies of the whole lifecycle expansion per trade-day. `Attribution` §3a and §3b instead work the chain in full on two legs whose value is closed form — a short MCX hedge across the day the LME broke from its all-time high, and a USD payable against the forward that hedges it — and check each step against `desk.mtm.curves` directly. That shows the mechanism honestly and proves the arithmetic; it does not claim to be the whole engine.

**Leg amounts from the ticket lifecycle.** A purchase invoice on an `LME_M1_AVG` formula is the mean of the official cash price over the panel days of a quotational month; a quality claim depends on a survey outcome and a penalty schedule; demurrage counts calendar days of dwell past a free period; an LC fee runs on a validity computed from a laycan and a presentation period. These are date-and-contract computations on a rolled panel calendar, not market arithmetic — which is why the workbook carries the amount and derives everything the *market* does to it (the FX conversion to settle date, the aggregation into cumulative P&L).

## 9. Limitations and open issues

1. **The marking grid is not every day.** Leg-level marks exist on 25 dates, not on all 164 book days. The `Equity_Curve` carries the engine's daily open-MTM and re-derives it from `MTM_Daily` only where the grid reaches; the difference column shows both. Per-trade cumulative P&L is reconciled at `WINDOW_END` and `HORIZON_END` as the brief requires.
2. **Green cells that feed forward.** The colour convention would be cleaner if nothing downstream ever depended on a pasted value. It does not hold for the carried lifecycle amounts, so those rows carry an explicit `amount_src` / `value_src` column rather than relying on the reader to remember.
3. **Rounding floors three of the tolerances.** The workbook reads the published CSVs, which are rounded for determinism (₹0.01 on money, 4 dp on prices). `tol_cashflow_inr`, `tol_usd_t` and part of `tol_grid_inr` exist only because of that; `Checks` says so row by row, and the observed differences sit one to three orders of magnitude inside every tolerance.
4. **Phase 3 book parameters are still served from code.** `config/params/book.yaml` does not exist; `Inputs` §4 lists the eleven keys the workbook uses from `desk.mtm.constants.BOOK_PARAM_FALLBACKS`, with their flags and verification status, separately from the register so the two can never be confused. Three of them carry PENDING verification.
5. **Recalculation is slow outside Excel.** Excel itself recalculates the file instantly; the `formulas` library takes minutes because it builds an explicit dependency graph over every cell. The reconciliation test therefore runs as one long test and reports its own wall time.
6. **The workbook inherits every Phase 0 caveat.** Freight levels and the lag-2 grade mix are hindsight reconstructions; USD/INR is an ECB cross, PENDING a measured comparison against FBIL; the MCX panel series is import parity, so bucket (b) is zero by construction and the mirror run — which is the only way to see a real cross-exchange basis — is a Python sensitivity, not a workbook sheet.
